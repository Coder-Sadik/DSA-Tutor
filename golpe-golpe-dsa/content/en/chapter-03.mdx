<Story>
It was the first day of the holidays. Dadu (their grandfather) had hidden a box of sweets for Rafi and Nila, but he had a rule: "No map. You only get the first clue."

The first clue was taped to the front door. It said: "Look under the shoe rack." Under the shoe rack was a second slip: "Go to the mango tree." On the tree was a third: "Check the water tank." Then the rooftop. And on the rooftop, tucked under a flowerpot, was the box of sweets.

Rafi was impatient. "Dadu, can I just go straight to the fourth clue?"

"You can't," Dadu laughed. "Nobody knows where the fourth clue is, except the third clue. You must follow the chain from the start."

Then Nila had an idea. "Dadu, what if you want to add one more stop, say the kitchen, between the mango tree and the water tank?"

"Easy," said Dadu. "I write a new slip that says 'Check the water tank', and I hide it in the kitchen. Then I change the mango tree's slip to say 'Go to the kitchen'. Nobody else has to move. Two small changes, and the hunt is longer."

"But be careful," he added. "If I change the mango tree's slip first and forget where the old one pointed, the rest of the hunt is lost forever."
</Story>

### Each clue only knows the next one

Unlike the lockers of Chapter 2, these clues are not in a row with numbers. They can be hidden anywhere. What connects them is that each slip tells you where the next one is.

<Definition term="Linked list">
A **Linked list** is a chain of items where each item (called a **node**) holds two things: its own value, and a link (**pointer**) to the next node. You can only enter the chain at its first node, called the **head**.
</Definition>

### Four Key Facts

* **No direct access:** To reach the 5th node you must follow 4 links from the head.
* **Grows easily:** New nodes can be created anywhere there is space; no fixed row size is needed.
* **Cheap to change the chain:** Once you are standing at the right node, adding or removing a node changes just one or two links. Nobody else moves.
* **Extra cost:** Every node must carry its link as well as its value.

<Remember title="Careful! (An Honest Note)">
Changing a link is cheap, but getting to the right spot is not. To insert after the 4th clue you first have to walk 4 hops. Linked lists win when you are already at the spot, or when you keep adding and removing at the front.
</Remember>

### Array vs Linked List

| Feature | Array (lockers) | Linked list (clues) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reach the 5th item** | 1 step | 4 hops from the start |
| **Insert in the middle** | Shifts many items | Change 1 or 2 links (after reaching the spot) |
| **Size** | Fixed row | Grows as needed |
| **Memory** | Only the values | Values plus a link per item |

### Real-World Uses

* **Train coaches:** A new coach is added in the middle by uncoupling one link and coupling two, without moving the other coaches.
* **"Next song" in a music player:** Each song knows which one plays next.
* **Photo viewers and slideshows:** Stepping forwards and backwards with next and previous buttons.
* **Browsing web links:** Hunting through a chain of web page links, one click at a time.
